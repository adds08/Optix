# The reporting line becomes a recorded fact, and the chart is a third window onto one roster

Urban's structure is deeper than the product's. The client describes
director → area in-charge → PM & general superintendent → superintendent → foreman,
their titles are decided per department rather than company-wide, and one person
routinely holds several of them at once — a PM who is also the area in-charge on the
one job in his patch. The request was an organisation chart, with the specific
requirement that changing a role on the Tools by Jobsite page shows up in it.

That requirement needed no mechanism. `tbl_ops_project_team_member` is already the one
place who-runs-a-job lives, and its two writers were deliberately unified through
`moveEmployeeToProject` so the jobsite hub and the people screen cannot disagree. The
chart is a third reader of the same rows: nothing is stored for it and nothing is
synchronised into it.

What was missing was the edge. The roster records that a person is on a job in a role;
it never recorded who they answer to.

## What changed

### `project_team_member.reports_to_employee_id` — the chart's only edge

Migration `0041_team_reports_to`. Nullable, `on delete set null`, indexed on the target
because the chart walks down ("who reports to X") far more than up.

An earlier plan in this session put a **rank** on the role register instead, so the tree
could be derived from "PM outranks superintendent". That was wrong and was withdrawn
before any of it was written: a rank asserts one company-wide ladder, and this is sold to
firms whose ladders genuinely differ. Recording the edge per row asserts nothing, and can
represent the messy shapes — an engineer over a superintendent, a foreman reporting
straight to a director on a small job — which is what the product has to track rather
than correct.

It points at an **employee**, not at another roster row, so the person named needs no row
of their own on that project. That is what lets one director sit above forty jobs without
forty rows restating it, and it is why the design needed no "area" entity.

This is not a revival of `employee.reportsToEmployeeId`, which was dropped from scoping on
2026-08-23. That column drifted precisely because it lived on the person, away from the
roster. This one lives on the roster row, so it cannot disagree with the row that says
where the person is working. The employee column is untouched and remains a People-screen
display field.

### `buildOrgForest`, `visibleEmployeeIds`, `findCycle` — pure, in `packages/domain`

The tree, the access rule and the cycle guard are one tested module, imported by both the
router and the browser. `apps/web` has no test runner at all — no test script, no test
files — so a tree-builder living in the page would have been untestable.

### `projectTeam.orgChart` — a separate procedure, scoped by person

Deliberately not a flag on `projectTeam.all`. `all` answers "who is on the jobs I can see"
and is scoped by project; this answers "who is in my reporting line" and is scoped by
person. A superintendent gets the PM above them and their own crew, and not the next
superintendent's crew — even though `all` shows both, because both supers are on the same
job. Who sees everything reuses the existing view ladder (`assets.view.all`) rather than
introducing a second idea of "admin".

The filtering is server-side. Sending every row and hiding some in the browser is not an
access rule.

### The chart page, at `/org-chart`

Tabs for the chart, a by-jobsite list and a "no reporting line" list; search across person,
job and role that expands the path and scrolls the card into view; jobsite and role
filters; collapse/expand and zoom. A person on several jobs is repeated under each parent
with an "also on N other jobs" chip that jumps between their instances, as asked.

### The seed grows a leadership layer

`seed-data.urban.ts` carried 26 foreman rows and no PM or superintendent at all, so the
team strip shipped in the previous session had nothing to draw. It now carries PMs and
superintendents for every job with a crew, all wired. Both datasets point their PMs at a
director who holds **no roster row**, because that is the case that removed the need for
an area entity and nothing would otherwise exercise it.

## What was found while building it

**The seed gap was half what it first appeared, and the first report of it was wrong.**
An initial check said neither dataset seeded a PM or superintendent row, and that the four
in the local database had been entered by hand. That check used a bad `awk` range and read
the wrong block. The demo fixture — the default dataset — has always seeded two PMs and two
superintendents, and those four rows are exactly the ones in the database. Only the urban
dataset was bare.

**`buildOrgForest` could emit a cyclic children graph.** With `a → b` and `b → a` it
produced `a.children = [b]` and `b.children = [a]`; the first recursive render never
returns. Caught by the test written for it, and fixed by breaking cycles before linking
rather than tolerating them afterwards — one edge cut per loop, so the rest of the chain
still renders. The write path refuses to create one at all; `routers/project.ts` already
rejected the depth-1 self-reference, but the case that bites is `A → B → C → A`, entered
one innocent row at a time by three different people, none of whom can see the whole chain.

**No charting library earned its place.** The plan named `d3-hierarchy` and it was not
installed. `buildOrgForest` already produces the nested shape, and what remained was boxes
and connector lines, which flexbox does natively. A layout library earns its place when you
need coordinates — for SVG, or for crossing edges. DOM cards additionally give the
browser's own text wrapping, `scrollIntoView` for the search jump, and real focusable
elements. React Flow was considered and rejected on the same reasoning plus a specific one:
its value is drag-connect editing, which this chart must not have.

**`apps/web` had never imported a multi-file workspace package at runtime.**
`@stinventory/api-contracts` is in `transpilePackages` but is imported for *types only*, so
it is erased before bundling and its `.js` re-export specifiers never had to resolve.
`@stinventory/types` is a single file with nothing to re-export. `@stinventory/domain` is
the first real runtime import, and the barrel's `./rules.js` specifiers — which tsx
resolves to `.ts` and the Next bundler does not — broke the build. Fixed with an
`./org-chart` subpath export pointing at a file that imports nothing. Adding an import to
`org-chart.ts` will re-break the web build and only the web build.

**Anonymous volumes are per service, and the trap fires between containers.** Installing
the new dependency inside `stinventory-api` left `stinventory-web` unable to resolve it:
each service declares its own `/workspace/node_modules`, so they are separate volumes.
`pnpm install` had to run in the web container too. `docker/Dockerfile.dev` needed no new
COPY line — `apps/web/package.json` is already in the list — and no new anonymous volume
was needed for `packages/domain`, which is mounted through `./packages` and already worked
that way for the api.

## Verified

- `pnpm typecheck` across the workspace: 13 of 13 tasks pass.
- `packages/domain` vitest: 44 tests pass, 12 of them new and covering same-project parent
  preference, the director-with-no-rows case, sibling exclusion in the visibility rule,
  cycle termination in all three functions, and the depth-3 cycle the old self-check missed.
- Migration applied against the local database; column, index and foreign key confirmed
  present with `\d tbl_ops_project_team_member`.
- `/org-chart` serves HTTP 200 from the running dev stack.

**Not verified.** The chart has not been driven in a browser and no screen of it has been
looked at, so its layout, the connector lines, the search jump and the tab behaviour are
unproven beyond compiling. The new seed rows are **not loaded**: `make seed` skips an
existing tenant and loading them needs `SEED_RESET=1`, which wipes the local database and
was not run without the owner's say-so. Every `reports_to` in the local database is
therefore still null, so the chart currently draws every person as their own root — the
hierarchy itself has never been rendered. No test covers `projectTeam.orgChart` or the
cycle guard at the router level; both are exercised only through the pure functions
beneath them.

## Deliberately not done

- **No UI writes the edge yet.** The column exists and `projectTeam.assign` accepts
  `reportsToEmployeeId`, but no control sets it — the team strip picker is not built, so
  today the edge can only arrive from the seed.
- **`TEAM_ROLES` is still the hardcoded `["pm", "superintendent", "foreman"]`.** Director,
  area in-charge and general superintendent cannot yet be assigned *as team roles*. The
  seeded director works because they hold no roster row at all. Making the vocabulary
  data-driven — `tbl_entity_company_role` already exists for exactly this, tenant-scoped
  and admin-editable — is the next piece and is what "tiers can expand later" actually
  requires.
- **Delegated assignment is unchanged.** The permission matrix still gates on the target
  role; "you may assign anyone who will report to you" was designed and not built, because
  it is the only part that alters an existing authorisation surface.
- **Departures do not re-point orphans.** When a PM's row closes, everyone beneath them
  loses their parent and re-roots. Re-pointing to the leaver's own parent belongs in
  `departure.ts`.
