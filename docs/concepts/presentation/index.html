Scope

Deliberately not built

A register that guesses is worse than one that admits what it does not know. Each of these was removed or never built, on purpose:

Rentals & loans

No rented-tool tracking, and no loan model with due dates. Neither is a custody fact Urban asked to manage.

Overdue & reminders

The borrow model and expected-return dates were deleted. Nothing goes "overdue" because nothing is borrowed.

Foreman-to-foreman hand-offs

A foreman cannot pass a tool to another foreman. Tools move through the equipment desk, or not at all.

Vendors, POs, cost codes

Purchasing documents and job-cost accounting are not this system's job.

Money on the field screen

Capital figures are desk reports. A foreman's screen does not carry dollars.

What is in scope

One register of small tools, custody of them, who holds what on which job — and the history of every move.

Bodhi Labs · Optix Small Tools InventoryScope
If asked "why can't a foreman just hand a tool to the next foreman?", the answer is the ledger: an unrecorded move is indistinguishable from a lost tool. Routing every move through the desk is what makes "who holds what" true.

Getting data in

Two doors, nothing invented

Things and people arrive by different doors — and neither door lets Optix make anything up.

Files carry things

Projects, equipment and small tools are imported from Urban's own files through the Import button on each register. What is missing stays missing; blank cells stay blank.

BambooHR carries people

People arrive from the live HR sync. Every one lands as crew with no login. Access is granted by hand, later, as a separate decision.

Nothing is seeded. An empty register is the correct starting state — approximate data is worse than none, because nothing on screen would say which rows to trust.
Bodhi Labs · Optix Small Tools InventoryData in
The point of this slide is trust: the register is only useful if every row came from a named source. Two sources, two doors, no invented rows.

Show the real headers, not a mock-up. The order matters because equipment and tools can resolve a project by name; load projects first and those references have something to land on. The preview/commit sharing one code path is the honest-import guarantee.

Getting data in

BambooHR is read-only

GET only

The sync takes no verb parameter at all, so no argument can make it write back to HR.

No logins created

Everyone lands as crew with no login. The people who need access are invited by hand, one at a time.

Departure is a flag

A termination reported by the sync is a flag for an administrator — never a write Optix performs on its own.

Title → role is manual

HR's job titles map to login roles in Settings → Job Titles. The sync never guesses which role somebody gets.

The source of truth stays HR

Optix reads people; it does not own them. Correcting a person means correcting BambooHR.

People carry no tools

Custody is a record about a tool, not a field on a person — so the sync and the register never fight.

Bodhi Labs · Optix Small Tools InventoryData in
The read-only claim is structural, not a promise: the client cannot construct a write. If somebody asks whether Optix could ever overwrite HR, the answer is that there is no verb to do it with.

Getting data in

Duplicates refused at the door

Three things must be unique before a row is allowed into the register.

Code

Unique per tenant, case-insensitive. TOOL-00001 and tool-00001 are the same tool.

Serial

Unique. A repeated serial refuses the whole file rather than merging two tools into one guess.

VIN

Unique on equipment. The same truck cannot enter twice under two codes.

A duplicate is refused, not reconciled. That is deliberate: a merge is a guess, and a guess in the register is worse than a failed import.
Bodhi Labs · Optix Small Tools InventoryData in
This is the answer to "what if the same tool is in two files?" It is refused, and the importer says which row collided. Nothing gets silently merged, so the register never contains two half-tools.

Daily work

Create a small tool

  • Small Tools → New tool. Make or description is required; everything else can be filled in later.
  • Leave Code blank and the system mints one: TOOL-00001, then TOOL-00002, and so on.
  • A duplicate code is refused: "… is already in the register." Nothing is merged, nothing is guessed.
  • The same code is generated on import, so all 753 rows arrive with IDs without anyone typing one.

Daily work

Attach tools by jobsite

Tools by Jobsite is the control hub: one card per job, with its crews and the tools working it.

  • Tick the loose tools on a job's card, then assign them to that crew in one action.
  • Drill down from job, to crew, to an individual tool's serial.
  • Gaps the desk chases: no crew · a crew with no truck · a crew with no trailer.
  • The field can just say it: a foreman describes the move in one sentence in chat — no syntax, no codes. The desk acts, and the ledger records it.
Attaching is how a tool stops being "somewhere" and becomes "with this crew, on this job".
Bodhi Labs · Optix Small Tools InventoryDaily work
The three gaps are the desk's actual daily queue: a job with no crew, a crew with no truck, a crew with no trailer. The screen surfaces them so they get chased rather than discovered at month end.

Daily work

Equipment state, honestly

No status field

Equipment has no status of its own. There is no "in service" to set and no "offline" to forget.

"Sent to site" means something

It means the unit is on a rig with a foreman, on a project — a fact, not a label somebody typed.

What moves is custody

Custody is recorded in the ledger. A unit's place is a projection of those records, not a field on the unit.

Two honest caveats. Import links equipment to jobs by exact project name, and today 0 of 88 rows carry a project link — so no slide here claims a count of "rigs on site". And every project date is a placeholder, so every job reads as permanently active; there is no timeline claim to make.
Bodhi Labs · Optix Small Tools InventoryDaily work
Say the caveats out loud rather than hiding them: equipment has no status by design, and the equipment-to-project link is not populated yet, so any "rigs on site" number would be fiction. Project dates are placeholders, so don't draw a timeline.

How Optix works

The custody lifecycle

Stage 1

Tag & register

A tool enters the register and gets a tool ID. A tag is optional until somebody labels it.

Stage 2

Assign to a custodian

Issued by the equipment desk and charged to a project. Custody is now on a person.

Stage 3

Reassign

The desk moves custody to another custodian or job. High-value tools need a second approver.

Stage 4

Yard return

Cleared back to the desk, or reassigned straight to the next job. The tool is available again.

Control: only the equipment desk moves tools — a foreman reports, never reassigns. A move above the high-value threshold parks for a second administrator's approval before it lands.
Bodhi Labs · Optix Small Tools InventoryCustody
Earlier versions of this story promised a 24-hour confirmation window and one-tap receiver sign-off. That was removed with the loan model. The current control is approval-by-value, not a timer.

How Optix works

One ledger, one writer

Custody is event-sourced. The transaction ledger is the system of record; what a tool "currently" is, is only a projection of that ledger.

Ledger
Every move, append-only
→
Projection
Current holder & location on the tool
→
Screens
Register, custody, jobsites, reports
  • One writer. A single custody module is the only thing that may write custody — no screen writes a holder directly.
  • Corrections are events. Fixing a mistake logs a correction; history is never deleted.
  • Every screen is a projection. If a screen and the ledger disagree, the ledger is right and the screen is a bug.
Bodhi Labs · Optix Small Tools InventoryHow it works
This is the single most important architectural slide. The projector concept is what lets the product answer "who held this on the 3rd?" and still drive a live register.

Feature

Custody — who is holding what

  • Ownership vs custody: who paid for a tool and who is holding it are tracked separately.
  • Held: in the hands of a named foreman, superintendent or the shop.
  • In transit: between jobs, or on its way back to the yard.
  • Awaiting approval: a high-value move parked for a second sign-off.
Where a tool is located is never typed manually — it is derived from an unbroken chain of custody records.

Roles & access

How access actually works

Three separate axes describe a person. Only one of them belongs on screen.

1 · HR job title

What payroll calls the post. Decides which login role a synced person arrives with.

2 · Login role

What an account may see and do. Set in Settings → Access Roles.

3 · Job tier

Where somebody sits on one project — foreman, superintendent, PM. Per project, never derived from a title.

The same person can be a Carpenter, hold no account at all, and be a Foreman on this job — all three at once, and all three are true. On screen they get one word: the one they would use for themselves.
Bodhi Labs · Optix Small Tools InventoryAccess model
Tier must stay per-project. Urban's own data has a superintendent on one job and a foreman on another; if tier were a property of the role, their crew scope on the second job would leak the first job's foremen. This was already fixed once — do not collapse them.
